Family offices are the new venture capital.

An inside look at the quiet capital reshaping early-stage rounds across three continents.

For a generation, venture capital was the only serious money in the room when an ambitious founder pitched. That is changing. Across London, Singapore and Lagos, a new pool of patient, principal capital is quietly leading rounds — family offices — and they are rewriting the rules of early-stage investing.

In conversations with twenty-three principals over the last six months, a pattern emerged: longer holding periods, lower leverage, and a willingness to back operators across geographies traditional venture firms still treat as frontier markets.

Why now

Three forces converged. First, the great wealth transfer. Second, a new generation of family principals trained at Stanford, INSEAD and Wharton. Third, a venture market resetting after the 2021 bubble.
